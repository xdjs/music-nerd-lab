Help me design and build my own camera-led music discovery experience, inspired by Music Nerd Lens.

Start a conversation, not a build. Ask one question at a time, wait for my answer, and use what I have already told you. Offer two or three concrete choices and an “I'm not sure” path. If I am unsure, suggest a small starting point and explain why.

First find out:
• What should people scan: records, posters, instruments, everyday objects, or something else?
• Who is it for, and what should they discover or do after scanning?
• What should a result feel like: a swipeable story, a listening trail, a collector's note, or my own idea?
• Which devices, languages and accessibility needs matter?
• What sources, brand assets and visual references do I have?
• What tools, model access, budget and coding experience do I have? Should saved results stay on the device or follow an account?

Do not turn this into a long questionnaire. Skip answered questions; ask a follow-up only when it changes the plan. After the essentials, summarize my idea, propose the smallest useful version and ask me to confirm before building.

Then work in stages:
1. Map capture/upload → identification → discovery → save/share. Include camera denial, uncertain identification and retry states. Explain where uploaded images go. Never identify a person by guessing from their appearance.
2. Agree how claims will be supported. Separate model-generated suggestions from verified facts. If I want researched results, add actual retrieval and source checks; a generated URL is not verification. Keep evidence beside each claim.
3. Inspect my existing design system and references. Propose one coherent visual direction with a sample result. Use my branding; do not assume I can reuse Music Nerd's assets.
4. Build the confirmed version with server-side model credentials, validated image input and responses, accessible controls and clear loading/error states. Choose the stack for my constraints. A quick first result followed by deeper requests is an option, not a requirement.
5. Review real rendered screens at phone and desktop widths. Test camera/upload, ambiguous images, long text, failed analysis, source links, and reopening saved results—including all additional facts. State which tests could not be performed.
6. Walk me through setup and deployment when I am ready. Ask before publishing, spending money or enabling external services.

Keep a short cookbook as we work: actual tools, meaningful decisions, changes after testing and remaining limitations. Do not claim database, Music Nerd API or account integration until supported contracts are checked and the flow works.

Begin with: “What would you like someone to point their camera at—and what would you love them to discover?”
